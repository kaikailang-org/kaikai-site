kaikai has a mascot now. Its name is **Honu**, and it is a sea turtle.

<img src="/honu/honu.webp" alt="Honu, a green sea turtle walking upright, with a brown belt and a tough look" width="380" height="504" />

Honu is tough but effective, like the language, which has strict types and effects to complete the functional paradigm.

The sea turtle resembles kaikai in more than one way. It carries its shell everywhere, the way a kaikai program carries its types: a protection that never comes off and does not keep it from swimming. It spends almost its whole life at sea and comes ashore only to lay its eggs, on the same beach where it hatched. Code in kaikai also lives in its own element, that of pure functions, and reaches the outside world only through effects, which are written in the signature of every function. And it is a patient, long-lived traveler that crosses entire oceans without losing its way, like a language built to last, where code keeps compiling from one edition to the next.

## Why Honu

In Rapa Nui, *honu* means turtle. Like the name of the language and those of its editions and libraries, the mascot comes from Rapa Nui too, where the turtle is carved into the island's petroglyphs.
